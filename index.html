HOW IT WAS MADE

Explore the construction of our flood-awareness model, the Assam flood and coal-mining scene, the main flood diagram, and the protection ideas represented by the SHIELD project.

A
STEP 01 • MODEL CONSTRUCTION

HOW WE BUILT THE MAIN MODEL

The main SHIELD model was built on a plywood base so that the complete landscape could remain strong and stable.

For the mountains and raised landforms, we used thermocol and shaped it carefully to create the required landscape. A layer of cement was used over selected parts to give the mountains a stronger and more realistic surface.

After the structure dried, we used spray paint and other colours to create the appearance of mountains, land, roads and surrounding areas.

We also drilled small holes at selected points of the model to represent drainage paths and water-flow locations. These details help demonstrate how water can move through the landscape during heavy rainfall.

MATERIALS USED
🪵 PLYWOOD ⬜ THERMOCOL 🧱 CEMENT 🎨 SPRAY COLOUR 🛠️ DRILL 💧 WATER
🏔️
BUILDING THE LANDSCAPE

Thermocol provided the basic shape, cement strengthened the surface, and colours were used to create the final landscape appearance.

B
STEP 02 • FLOOD & COAL MINING

THE FLOOD MODEL

A second part of the exhibition model represents a landscape affected by flooding and coal-mining activity. It helps us show how human activity, landform changes and water can interact with the surrounding environment.

For this section, we used aluminium foil, tissue paper, thermocol, water and pieces of coal to create different textures and landscape features.

The aluminium foil was shaped to represent uneven surfaces and water-flow areas. Tissue and thermocol were used to build the terrain and surrounding structures. Real coal pieces were placed in the mining area to make the scene easier to identify.

Water was added to demonstrate how floodwater can spread through low-lying areas and interact with the model's landscape.

⛏️
COAL-MINING AREA LAND • WATER • COAL

The coal pieces represent the mining zone, while the surrounding terrain shows how water can move through and around altered land surfaces.

🌉
THE BRIDGE

A small bridge was added to the model to make the landscape more realistic and to represent infrastructure that can be affected when floodwater rises.

MATERIALS USED

STEP 03 • FLOOD DIAGRAM

WHERE THE FLOOD HAPPENS

The diagram explains the main point where flooding develops. During periods of heavy rainfall, a large amount of water enters rivers and drainage systems.

When the amount of incoming water becomes greater than the capacity of the river channel or drainage system, the water level rises.

Once the river reaches its bank or the drainage system cannot carry the excess water, the water can overflow into nearby low-lying areas.

STEP 04 • SHIELD RESPONSE

PREVENTION & PROTECTION

The final part of the SHIELD project focuses on reducing the impact of floods through awareness, preparation and early action.

Proper drainage can help move excess water away from vulnerable areas. Protecting natural water bodies and keeping drainage channels clear can also improve the movement of floodwater.

Early warning systems and community awareness can give people more time to prepare, move to safer locations and protect important belongings.

Better planning, suitable flood-control structures and protection of the natural environment can help reduce flood risk.
